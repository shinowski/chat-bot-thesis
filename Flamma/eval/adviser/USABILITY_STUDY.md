# Human usability study — participant testing pending

Browser automation verifies layout and controls; it does not measure user satisfaction, comprehension, accessibility with assistive technology, or actual task success.

Recruit intended users under the project's consent/ethics protocol. Use synthetic cases and non-identifying images. Record participant IDs without personal health data. Predefine sample size and devices before recruitment; do not claim a representative sample from a convenience pilot.

Tasks: begin screening; correct location/duration; ask a follow-up; upload a test photo; explain the score and uncertainty in the participant's own words; recover from rejection; start a different concern; find and export a conversation; delete it. Include mobile and desktop devices, keyboard-only navigation and screen-reader review.

Record task completion, assistance, time, mistakes, abandonment, perceived clarity and confidence. Ask whether a screening suggestion is a confirmed diagnosis, whether normal skin excludes disease, and whether chats automatically retrain the model. Use a validated usability questionnaire such as SUS according to its published scoring protocol; do not invent a score without participant responses.

Suggested CSV fields: participant_id,device,viewport,task,completed,assisted,seconds,error_count,comments. Report counts and denominators, median and range, failures and qualitative themes. Keep participant results separate from automated response-time and browser measurements.
